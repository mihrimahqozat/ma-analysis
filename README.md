# ma-analysis
A SQL and Python analysis of 1,455 acquisitions by major technology companies — Apple, Google, Microsoft, Facebook, Amazon, and others — spanning several decades, examining deal volume trends, sector focus, geographic reach, valuation multiples, and acquirer strategy profiles.

## Key Findings
- **Disney generated $113.9B in total deal value
  from just 19 acquisitions** ($8.14B avg) —
  the highest total value of any acquirer,
  driven by the $71.3B 21st Century Fox deal,
  demonstrating the transformative acquisition
  strategy vs high-frequency approaches
- **Google averaged 12.20 deals per year** — the
  highest cadence — at just $172.7M avg deal size,
  reflecting a systematic acqui-hire and early
  stage technology absorption strategy aligned
  with defending its advertising dominance
- **2014 was the peak M&A year at 100 deals**
  — coinciding with the height of mobile computing
  and cloud infrastructure acquisition waves as
  companies raced to compete in the post-PC era
- **Microsoft's $26.2B LinkedIn acquisition (2016)**
  stands as its most strategically significant deal,
  providing the professional network layer that
  underpins Microsoft 365 and Azure enterprise
  products
- **Apple made the smallest average deals ($134.4M)**
  among major acquirers despite being the world's
  most valuable company — acquiring specific
  technical capabilities (maps, sensors, AI) rather
  than entire businesses or market share
- **Twitter's $3.70B average deal size is a data
  quality anomaly** — the $50.1B Periscope value
  is almost certainly a data entry error (reported
  at ~$100M), highlighting the data validation
  challenge in M&A price datasets
- **72.7% of geo-tagged deals target U.S. companies**
  — United Kingdom (17), Canada (14), and Israel (6)
  lead international targets, with Israel showing
  disproportionate deal activity relative to
  population in cybersecurity and enterprise software
- **Only 26.3% of deals have disclosed prices**
  — valuation analysis covers a selective subset
  of larger transactions, and true total M&A
  spend significantly exceeds disclosed figures
